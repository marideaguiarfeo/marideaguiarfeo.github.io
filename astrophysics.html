Astrophysics Research AssistantJun 2026 – present

HST Analysis of Type II Supernovae & Host Environments

With Dr. Charlie Kilpatrick · CIERA at Northwestern University

Since June 2026 I have been working with Dr. Charlie Kilpatrick on a project to analyze Type IIP Supernovae Stellar environment using data from an HST proposal that parsed around 90 targets. I worked on processing the data for these targets, including all steps from retriving data from MAST the catalog to running photometric analysis. I big part of my job was troubleshooting Dr. Kilpatrick's existing pipeline for HST data, called hst123, and helping him find and solve erros in run and alignment. Additionally, I analyzed the resulting data for this reduction. I developed code to plot color magnitude diagrams, fit isochrone and evolutionary tracks, and estimate progenitor star information for each target. This code can be found here.. Now I am helping troubleshoot st123, a new unified pipeline for Space Telescopes designed to work for HST, JWST, and (hopefully soon) for Roman. Using this new pipeline, I am rerunning data reduction for previous hST targets that had alignment errors. Next steps will be analyzing each Target's individual environment, retriving information about it's progenitor and contextualizing findings in existing literature and theory.

Astrophysics Research AssistantJan 2025 – present

Chemical Dynamics of the Milky Way

With Dr. Andreia Carillo · Carleton College

This research project focuses on using low-resolution spectra (R ~ 3000) to analyze the kinematics and chemistry of a set of ~100 red clump stars at > 10 kpc in the halo-disk area of the Milky Way Galaxy. Our primary objective is to, based on their chemical abundances, determine their stellar population classification. This will contribute to a broader goal to characterize the disk–halo interface, where both Milky Way stars and accreted populations coexist. My main task has been reducing the project’s data using the CURE reduction pipeline for VIRUS-W integral field unit to fix errors in the existing reduction. Rerunning and troubleshooting existing errors within this pipeline included tasks such as sorting and organizing the fits files by data type, writing Python scripts, and investigating/modifying existing scripts. The next step is to compare the reduction results and analyze particular spectral features sensitive to magnesium and sodium abundances, which are tracers key to distinguish between thin disk, thick disk, halo, and accreted stellar populations at low spectral resolution.

Research AssistantSept 2025 – present

Physics Education Outreach Project

With Dr. Marty Baylor · Carleton College

This is a Physics Education and Outreach project of designing a week long physics course for children that are part of the Puerta Abierta Project in Santiago de Atitlán, Guatemala. I am leading the design of a 2-day astronomy component for children ages 7–10 with theoretical and hands-on observational activities. I have also surveyed available children's teaching materials in Spanish on physics and astronomy and will design materials for astronomy and archaeoastronomy learning in Spanish and Tz'utujil (ancient indigenous Mayan language) to facilitate the children’s learning. The project will be carried out in person in Guatemala in Summer 2027.
